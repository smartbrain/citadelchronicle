### Obituary 1: Ward Christensen (1945–2024)
**Headline**: **THE SYSOP’S FINAL LOGOFF: WARD CHRISTENSEN (1945–2024)**  
**Subhead**: *The man who gave us XMODEM and the world’s first Bulletin Board System proved that community does not require corporate clouds.*

On 11 October 2024, the global computing underground lost one of its truest sovereign architects. Ward Christensen passed away at his home in Illinois at the age of seventy-eight. 

To modern internet users raised in the sterile, algorithmic walled gardens of Meta, Google, and ByteDance, Christensen’s name may evoke little recognition. But to anyone who remembers the crackle and hiss of an acoustic coupler in the dark of night, Ward was a digital Prometheus. 

During the historic Great Blizzard of Chicago in January 1978, as snowdrifts buried the city, Christensen and his collaborator Randy Suess set to work with an S-100 computer, a Hayes modem, and a single copper telephone wire. Within four weeks, they brought online **CBBS (Computer Bulletin Board System)**—the world’s very first computerized BBS. For the first time, ordinary citizens could dial into an independent local node, leave public messages, exchange code, and form self-governing electronic communities completely outside the control of corporate monopolies or state telecommunications ministries. 

A year earlier, in 1977, Christensen had solved another fundamental dilemma of the analog era: he created **XMODEM**. By breaking binary files into 128-byte packets protected by a single checksum, XMODEM allowed software to be transferred across noisy, unreliable copper telephone circuits without corruption. It was simple, robust, unpatented, and given away freely to the world.

Long before Tim Berners-Lee linked hypertext, before Mark Zuckerberg privatised friendship, and decades before Nostr returned us to the wisdom of independent relays, Ward Christensen proved that the purest digital networks are open, asynchronous, and built by hobbyists who simply refuse to wait for permission.

*Sysop Christensen has dropped carrier for the final time. May his parity bits remain forever uncorrupted.*

### Obituary 2 (Satirical Autopsy): The Thai 10,000-Baht "Blockchain" Token (2023–2024)
**Headline**: **IN PEACEFUL REPOSE: THE THAI STATE BLOCKCHAIN (2023–2024)**  
**Subhead**: *Born in a campaign PowerPoint, terrified by fiscal laws, and buried at a Krungthai ATM.*

It is with heavy hearts and stifled giggles that *The Citadel Chronicle* records the formal demise of the Kingdom’s most ambitious digital experiment: the **10,000-Baht Digital Wallet Utility Token**. 

Born in the spring of 2023 amidst deafening electoral fanfare, the deceased was celebrated by ruling party technocrats as a revolutionary leap in sovereign monetary theory. It was promised to be a bespoke, open-loop, geofenced programmable cryptographic asset, restricted to transactions within a four-kilometre radius of the citizen's registered household, capable of igniting a nationwide economic tsunami through the sheer sorcery of distributed ledgers.

Alas, contact with reality proved fatal. 

First came the auditors from the State Audit Office, who noted that geofencing human commerce with smartphone GPS was mathematically and constitutionally unhinged. Then came the Ministry of Finance, who quietly observed that building a bespoke national blockchain to hand out pocket money would cost more than the pocket money itself. 

Weakened by bureaucratic panic, the token was placed into palliative care throughout the wet season of 2024. The plug was finally pulled last month when Prime Minister Paetongtarn Shinawatra announced the scheme’s final, pathetic reincarnation: 10,000 baht dispensed in crumpled, paper 1,000-baht notes via PromptPay directly to Krungthai bank cards.

The deceased leaves behind five million unused PowerPoint slides, sixty-four confused ministry press conferences, and a bewildered population that discovered the ultimate end of all state blockchain projects is a long queue at an ATM.

*No flowers by request. Donations of paper baht may be deposited directly into our local coffee fund.*
